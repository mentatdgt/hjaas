# 013 — Logo: six new ideas, real metal pin on white

## What we learned from 012
- **Keep the metal.** It finally read as real: chrome on the bell, polished metal next to matte, see-through enamel over engraved texture. Keep that look exactly.
- **Change the ideas.** None was quite "it".
- **No people or faces of any kind.** The robot and the portraits are out.

## The brand
**HJaaS = "Hui Jie as a Service."** A witty spoof of tech "Software as a Service" naming. Hui Jie is an interior designer who does everything for her friends: carpentry and renovation, interior design, video production, mushroom farming and travel.

The logo should be **punchy, witty and collectible**: one clever object with a bold silhouette, in mid-century retro-futurist style (the 1950s–60s space age as imagined then). It should be grown-up, never cute or childish.

**Original only.** Evoke the era, not any real brand, product or franchise. No real oil-company, hotel or lamp-maker designs, logos or lettering.

## The look: photograph of a real metal pin (keep exactly what worked in 012)
Make each option look like a **macro product photograph of a real, premium die-struck pin**, not a 3D render, sticker or illustration:
- **Real metal:**
  - Polished plating has high-contrast reflections. Most of the surface mirrors a dark room, with a few crisp bright bands from the light, so it looks like metal and not paint.
  - Choose the plating that suits each design: polished gold, bright chrome or nickel, or black nickel. Domes, globes and reels suit chrome.
- **Mixed finishes on every pin**, which is what makes premium pins look expensive:
  - Polished raised lines.
  - Some areas of bare sandblasted (matte, fine-grain) metal next to polished metal.
  - At least one area of **translucent enamel over engraved metal** (a sunburst or wave texture glowing through the colour, like jewellery).
  - Optionally, one small area of glitter enamel, e.g. flames or stars.
- **Soft enamel, no epoxy dome:**
  - Colour fills sit slightly recessed below crisp metal walls, with a semi-gloss surface and a tiny meniscus at the edges.
  - No thick clear coating and no white highlight blobs.
- **Crisp, thin edges:** sharp bevelled metal lines, and visible edge thickness of ~1.5 mm with a polished rim. Nothing puffy or rounded like plastic.
- **Photography: white studio, with metal that still looks like metal:**
  - Shot straight on, the pin lying flat on a **clean white seamless background**, so we see how the logo reads on a light page.
  - Light it the way product photographers shoot chrome on white: one large soft light from the upper left, plus **black cards just out of frame**. The black cards give the polished metal crisp dark reflection bands, so it never goes flat yellow-grey or plastic.
  - A tiny, soft contact shadow under the pin. Sharp focus across the whole pin.
- **Colour:** punchy and limited. Use the plating plus at most three enamel colours from cream `#FBF3EA`, tomato red `#D9483B`, mustard `#E9B949`, retro teal `#2E8C86`, deep navy `#1F2A44`, ink `#2A1B14`. Strong contrast between neighbouring colours.

## Lettering
- Give each option its own lettering to suit the idea: engraved small caps, chunky 1950s sign letters, chrome inline letters, or a ticket-stub typewriter face. Don't use the same face everywhere.
- The object and its silhouette carry the logo; "HJaaS" can be secondary in size.
- **Spelling is exactly** `HJaaS` (capital H, capital J, lowercase a a, capital S), plus `FULL SERVICE` and `No. 001` where used. Letters must be crisp and correct.
